# DUT Call 2026: Call Text Summary

Source: [`call-docs/DUT_Call_2026_Call_Text.pdf`](call-docs/DUT_Call_2026_Call_Text.pdf) (149 pages; text in `call-docs/DUT_Call_2026_Call_Text.txt`).
This is the fifth call of the Driving Urban Transitions (DUT) Partnership, co-funded under Horizon Europe (GA 101069506), and part of Mission Innovation (MICall26).

## Money
- About **EUR 49 M** from national and regional agencies, plus EC co-funding of up to 30%.
- Each agency funds **its own** applicants under its own rules (Annex A). There is no transnational minimum or maximum project size.
- Typical consortium: requests to **3–5 funding agencies**.
- **Oversubscription cap:** an agency's applicants are invited to stage 2 only up to 4× its budget (3× for agencies outside EC co-funding, marked AP: Finland and the Philippines). Avoid agencies that are small or popular.

## Transnational eligibility rules (failing any one makes the proposal ineligible)
1. At least **3 eligible applicants from 3 participating countries**. At least **2 of them must be from EC co-fund countries** (every participating country except Finland and the Philippines). Legal entities must be independent of each other.
2. At least **one urban public authority** (city, municipality, or an entity owned by the local government that provides key urban services). It can be a Main Applicant, Co-applicant or Cooperation Partner.
3. The **Main Applicant must be eligible for funding and must request it** from its own agency.
4. A **Principal Contact Point (PCP)** can be in **at most 2 proposals**, and lead the Main Applicant in only 1. Some agencies are stricter.
5. **Cross-cutting topic:** at least one **funded private-sector partner**.
6. **Maximum duration: 36 months.**
7. Written in **English**, on the **mandatory template**, within the page limits, with every section filled in.
8. Submitted via **UDiManager** before the deadline.
9. Only invited pre-proposals can go on to stage 2.
10. **No fundamental changes** between the pre-proposal and the full proposal. Objectives, Main Applicant and its PCP, and consortium stay the same; funding per agency can change only slightly. Changes requested by the Call Secretariat (the Inclusion or Fallback procedures) are exempt.

**Recommendations, not rules:** include civil society, and for the cross-cutting topic a funded civil-society partner. Keep the consortium balanced across countries. **No partner should have more than 50% of total person-months.** Every partner needs a clear role and should show how it will use the results.

## Two R&I approaches (choose one; you're ranked within it)
| | **ROA** (research-oriented) | **IOA** (innovation-oriented) |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Knowledge, data, modelling, tools for decision makers | Practical solutions: pilots, demonstrations, scaling, commercialisation |
| Academic publications | Very important | Encouraged |
| Empirical evaluation | Encouraged | **Required** |
| Stakeholders | At least advisors | **Directly involved in the work** |
| Urban Living Labs | Welcome | Encouraged |
| Valorisation | Useful insights and facts for decision makers | **Market potential** / response to a demand |
| Rough TRL equivalent | 1–4 | >4 |

The call **puts extra emphasis on IOA**. Strategic (fundamental) research is eligible only for topics 2 and 3 (Annex C). The panel produces **6 ranking lists**: each pathway (15mC / CUE / PED) × ROA or IOA.

## Topics
Select **one primary topic**; it decides which ranking list you're in and what you're evaluated against. You can add secondary topics.

**Topic 1, cross-cutting (IOA only): Adaptive reuse of existing urban structures and spaces.** Pick one main pathway, but combine aspects from **at least 2 of the 3 pathways**. A funded private partner is required. Expected outputs: real-world pilots, validated implementation models, evidence on costs, benefits and trade-offs, replicable toolkits, policy recommendations, participatory frameworks, and scaling strategies.

| Pathway | Topic 2 | Topic 3 |
|---|---|---|
| **15mC**, 15-minute City (mobility, proximity) | **Align ambitions for mobility transitions across sectors**: corporate mobility management, commuting, shared parking and charging infrastructure, hybrid work, links to sustainable urban mobility plans (SUMPs), Climate City Contracts and the EU sustainability reporting rules for companies (CSRD) | **Nurture neighbourhood economies**: local commerce, services and small-scale production, anchor institutions, community wealth building, ground-floor management, "policy packaging" |
| **CUE**, Circular Urban Economies (resources, greening) | **Innovative maintenance of urban green and blue spaces**: maintenance regimes that support biodiversity and climate resilience, governance and financing of upkeep, citizen stewardship, the Nature Restoration Law | **Rethinking urban production**: urban farming, repair and maker spaces, waste streams as inputs, zoning and permitting, circular production hubs |
| **PED**, Positive Energy Districts (energy) | **PED solutions in private housing**: deep renovation, heating and cooling, fragmented ownership, financing, energy poverty, New European Bauhaus | **Flexibility and integrated technologies**: multi-carrier flexibility, storage, demand response, V2G, grid congestion, digital twins, interoperability, local flexibility markets. Build on the PED projects funded in 2022 |

Every topic asks for **systemic** rather than isolated technical solutions, and for outputs that are impact- and process-oriented and user-centred.

## Cross-cutting expectations (section 2.1; evaluators look for these)
- An **urban focus** (peri-urban counts; links to rural areas are fine if the city stays central).
- **Stakeholder engagement** with demonstrated user needs, from co-design onwards, using a transdisciplinary, co-creative approach.
- A **systemic approach** covering the 4 impact dimensions: *culture and values · governance and policy · technologies and infrastructure · business models and tools*.
- A **place-based** approach.
- **Diversity and gender** in the consortium **and** in the R&I content. If it doesn't apply, explain why.
- **Transnational added value.**
- **Building on earlier work**: H2020 and Horizon Europe, the Cities Mission, JPI Urban Europe, NetZeroCities, LIFE, the Climate Adaptation Mission, the New European Bauhaus, CETPartnership.
- Outputs aimed at decision-making. Plans for dissemination, long-term uptake and scaling, and links to local strategies such as Climate City Contracts.

## Evaluation (pre-proposal = criteria without *)
Three criteria, each scored 0–5 (half points allowed). **To be funded: at least 3 on each criterion and at least 10 in total.** At least 3 experts review each pre-proposal.

| Criterion | Pre-proposal sub-criteria | Full proposal only (*) |
|---|---|---|
| **Excellence** | Clarity and relevance of objectives · credibility and soundness of the concept · transnational added value · ROA: originality, beyond the state of the art / IOA: innovativeness compared with business as usual | Methodology and feasibility · risks and mitigation |
| **Impact and user engagement** | Relevance to the call topic(s) and their expected outcomes · **diversity and gender** (or why not applicable) · stakeholder engagement · dissemination and exploitation | ROA: useful knowledge / IOA: market potential |
| **Implementation** | Consortium expertise, interdisciplinarity and co-creation · complementarity and balance of partners · feasibility of the timescale · **value for money** | Cost justification, tasks and workloads, management and risk, ethics and regulation, data management plan |

## Obligations if funded (plan the budget for them now)
- **Consortium agreement** within 6 months of the start, or **before** the start if enterprises are involved. The DESCA model is a good basis.
- **Annual progress report** in English, a KPI survey, annual popular summaries, and a public final report.
- **Up to EUR 30,000** reserved for programme activities (travel and time): kick-off 2027, mid-term 2028, final event 2029/30, plus at least 1 DUT event a year. Municipal partners join the **DUT City Panel**.
- **Knowledge Hub:** one named expert with **at least 5 person-months**, a mandatory work package, and deliverables at M12, M18 (input for the innovation portfolio), M24 and M36.
- **Mission Innovation (Urban Transitions Mission):** nominate a city representative, about **2 person-months** (optional but encouraged).
- **Communication plan** in year 1, with a named communication ambassador and its own budget.
- **Open science / FAIR:** open access to outputs and a data management plan (Annex B). Acknowledge DUT, DG RTD and the funding agencies in outputs.
- **DNSH:** no significant harm to the 6 EU Taxonomy environmental objectives.
- **AI use** must be disclosed: verify AI content and citations, list sources, check for plagiarism, follow the EC guidelines on generative AI.

**Tension to manage:** TA CR funds only applied research and innovation at TRL 3–9, with concrete result types such as a prototype, software or a certified methodology. ## How the funding works (no matching multiplier)
- There is **no "bring X and it gets multiplied" mechanism.** Each funded partner asks **its own national agency** for money for **its own eligible costs**. The agency pays a **funding rate** (a percentage of those costs).
- **Funding rate** depends on organisation type and research category:
  - Universities, public research organisations, and usually public authorities and non-economic NGOs: often **up to 100%**.
  - Companies (under state-aid rules): industrial/applied research **50–80%**, experimental development/innovation **25–60%**, depending on size. **The company pays the rest itself.**
- **"Total project costs" vs "Requested funds"** in the template: the difference is the partners' **own contribution**, including any cooperation partners who pay their own way.
- **Some agencies require a contribution** even from 100%-funded partners: **SIA at least 20% in-kind**; **SWEA/Viable Cities at least 50%** co-financing (75% for demonstration).
- **The EC's "up to 30% co-fund" goes to the agencies**, increasing the overall call budget. It doesn't increase any applicant's grant.
- **"4×" is the oversubscription cap**: an agency's applicants go to stage 2 only while their requests stay under about 4× its budget. It isn't a multiplier of your money.
